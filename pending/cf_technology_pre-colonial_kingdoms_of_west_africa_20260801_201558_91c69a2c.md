# Pre-Colonial Kingdoms Of West Africa

The pre-colonial kingdoms of West Africa, particularly in Ghana and other parts of the continent, were complex and sophisticated societies that thrived for centuries before the arrival of European colonizers. These kingdoms were characterized by rich cultural heritage, advanced systems of governance, and thriving economies. In this guide, we will delve into the history and practices of these kingdoms, highlighting their processes, methods, and frameworks.

**Introduction to Pre-Colonial Kingdoms**

The pre-colonial kingdoms of West Africa were a collection of powerful and influential states that existed from the 4th to the 19th century. These kingdoms were located in the savannas and forests of West Africa, stretching from modern-day Senegal to Nigeria. Some of the most notable pre-colonial kingdoms in Ghana and West Africa include:

1. The Ghana Empire (300-1200 AD)
2. The Mali Empire (1230-1465 AD)
3. The Songhai Empire (1465-1591 AD)
4. The Ashanti Kingdom (1670-1902 AD)
5. The Dahomey Kingdom (1600-1904 AD)

**Governance and Political Structure**

The pre-colonial kingdoms of West Africa had well-organized systems of governance, with monarchs, chiefs, and councils playing important roles. The monarch, often referred to as the king or emperor, was the supreme ruler of the kingdom. The monarch's powers were often checked by a council of elders or chiefs, who advised on important matters of state.

In the Ashanti Kingdom, for example, the monarch was known as the Asantehene, and was considered the embodiment of the state. The Asantehene was advised by a council of elders, known as the Asanteman Council, which was composed of senior chiefs and nobles.

**Economic Systems**

The pre-colonial kingdoms of West Africa had thriving economies, based on agriculture, trade, and craftsmanship. The kingdoms were major producers of goods such as gold, salt, and cloth, which were traded extensively throughout the region.

In the Ghana Empire, for example, the economy was based on the trade of gold, salt, and other precious commodities. The empire's strategic location at the crossroads of the trans-Saharan trade route made it an important hub for commerce.

**Social Structure**

The pre-colonial kingdoms of West Africa had complex social structures, with different classes and castes. The monarch and nobles formed the upper class, while farmers, artisans, and traders made up the middle class. Slaves and captives formed the lower class.

In the Dahomey Kingdom, for example, the social structure was divided into three main classes: the royalty, the nobles, and the commoners. The royalty and nobles held significant power and influence, while the commoners made up the majority of the population.

**Cultural Practices**

The pre-colonial kingdoms of West Africa had rich cultural practices, including music, dance, and art. The kingdoms were known for their vibrant festivals, which were often held to celebrate important events such as harvests, births, and coronations.

In the Mali Empire, for example, the cultural practice of griotism was highly valued. Griots were traveling musicians and storytellers who played an important role in preserving the empire's history and cultural heritage.
