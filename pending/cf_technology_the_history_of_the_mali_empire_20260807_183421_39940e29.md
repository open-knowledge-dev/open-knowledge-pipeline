# The History Of The Mali Empire

The Mali Empire, which flourished from the 13th to the 16th century, was a powerful and influential West African empire that played a significant role in the development of Ghanaian and African traditions, practices, and knowledge systems. **Founding and Expansion**
The Mali Empire was founded by Sundiata Keita, a legendary king who united various Mandinka clans and expanded the empire's borders through a series of military campaigns. At its peak, the empire stretched from the Atlantic coast to the Niger River, covering present-day Mali, Senegal, Gambia, Guinea, and parts of Sierra Leone and Liberia. The empire's expansion was facilitated by its strategic location, which enabled it to control the trans-Saharan trade routes and access the rich resources of the Niger River valley.

**Islamic Influences**
The Mali Empire was heavily influenced by Islam, which was introduced to the region by Arab traders and scholars. Many Malian rulers, including Sundiata Keita, converted to Islam, which became a unifying force for the empire.
