**Observation of Natural Phenomena**

In Ghana, local communities observe natural phenomena to predict weather patterns. For example:

1. **Changes in temperature**: Communities observe changes in temperature to predict the onset of the rainy season. A sudden drop in temperature often indicates the approach of rain.
2. **Humidity**: High humidity is often associated with impending rain, while low humidity indicates dry weather.
3. **Wind direction**: Changes in wind direction can indicate changes in weather patterns. For example, a shift in wind direction from the north to the south may indicate the approach of rain.
4. **Plant and animal behavior**: Communities observe the behavior of plants and animals to predict weather patterns. For example, the flowering of certain plants may indicate the onset of the rainy season, while the migration of certain animals may indicate the approach of dry weather.

**Traditional Weather Prediction Methods**

In Ghana, local communities use various traditional methods to predict weather patterns, including:

1. **Moon phases**: Communities use the phases of the moon to predict weather patterns. For example, the full moon is often associated with heavy rainfall, while the new moon is associated with dry weather.
2. **Star observations**: Communities observe the position and brightness of stars to predict weather patterns. For example, the appearance of certain stars may indicate the onset of the rainy season.
3. **Cloud observations**: Communities observe cloud formations to predict weather patterns. For example, cumulus clouds may indicate fair weather, while nimbus clouds may indicate rain.
4. **Smoke observations**: Communities observe the behavior of smoke from fires to predict weather patterns. For example, smoke that rises quickly and disperses may indicate fair weather, while smoke that hangs low to the ground may indicate rain.
