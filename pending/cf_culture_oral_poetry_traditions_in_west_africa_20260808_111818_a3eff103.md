# Oral Poetry Traditions In West Africa

Oral poetry traditions in West Africa represent a rich and complex cultural heritage that is deeply rooted in African thought and philosophical underpinnings. In African thought, words were considered to be more than mere sounds; they were believed to carry the essence of creation, and thus had the power to shape reality. This belief is reflected in the proverbs, riddles, and epics that form the basis of oral poetry traditions in Ghana and other West African countries.

One of the most prominent features of West African oral poetry is its use of metaphor and symbolism. These poetic devices enable the expression of complex ideas and emotions, often in a compact and memorable form. For example, the Ashanti people of Ghana have a proverb that says, "Anansi put his head in a calabash and it became a drum." This proverb, which refers to the trickster Anansi, conveys the idea that one's actions can have unexpected consequences, transforming ordinary objects into something new and meaningful.
